ENGINEERING EDUCATION

Study areas: from knowledge to practical ideas

Engineering brings knowledge from different subjects together to solve practical problems. STEM activities encourage students to ask questions, investigate, test ideas, and explain what they have learned.

⏱

AFTER REGULAR CLASSES

An extra hour of STEM every day

After regular lessons, students have a required additional one-hour session focused on STEM subjects. It gives them time to reinforce what they have learned and apply it to practical activities.

KEY AREAS

Knowledge that works together

These connected areas form part of an engineering and STEM approach. A single project can draw on knowledge from several of them.

01

Mathematics and natural sciences

Mathematics, physics, and other sciences help students understand engineering problems. They measure, compare, calculate, and draw conclusions from observations.

This knowledge helps test technical ideas, estimate outcomes, and explain why a solution works.

02

Engineering and design

Engineering thinking turns an idea into a plan and a working solution. It involves defining a problem, considering different options, building a model, and testing it.

Challenges during testing are part of the process: studying them helps improve a project.

03

Programming and digital technology

Programming teaches students to break a complex task into smaller steps, arrange them in a logical sequence, and check the result. Digital tools can also help present an idea and share project outcomes.

Web design, coding, and creative use of technology are examples of this area.

04

Experiments and research

Experiments help students use evidence to answer questions. They form a hypothesis, observe, record results, and check their conclusions.

In chemistry, physics, and other sciences, safety, careful observation, and clear explanations of results all matter.

The classroom and laboratory images are visualizations of a school environment.

HOW A PROJECT TAKES SHAPE

From a question to a presentation

Hands-on learning helps students find an answer and explain how they reached it.

  1. 1

    Define the question

    Understand the problem and decide what the project should solve or demonstrate.

  2. 2

    Plan a solution

    Choose relevant knowledge, compare ideas, and map out the steps.

  3. 3

    Test and improve

    Try out the model or solution, study the result, and refine it when needed.

  4. 4

    Present the result

    Explain the work clearly, support conclusions with evidence, and listen to the team’s feedback.

Engineering starts with curiosity

Mathematics, science, technology, and creativity come together to shape new solutions. Every student can develop engineering thinking by asking questions, exploring, and testing ideas.
